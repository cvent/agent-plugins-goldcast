# Blog post

Read this after you've chosen one angle (SKILL.md Step 5). The fidelity rules in Step 6 apply to everything here.

## Structure

1. **Title options.** Give 3, different in approach (a claim, a question, a how-to). Keep each under about 65 characters. The angle belongs in the title; the event name usually doesn't.
2. **Meta description.** One, 150 to 160 characters, plain language, stating what the reader will learn.
3. **Introduction (80 to 120 words).** Open with the reader's problem or the tension from the notes. State the angle and what the reader will get. Don't open with filler like "In today's fast-paced world".
4. **Body (3 to 5 sections with H2 headings).** Each section makes one point from the notes and supports it with what a speaker actually said or showed: a number (attributed to the speaker), an example, or a short verbatim quote. Write headings as statements of the point, not labels like "Overview". Don't walk through the session in chronological order.
5. **Takeaways (optional).** 3 to 5 short bullets, each restating something a speaker recommended or demonstrated. No advice of your own.
6. **Closing and CTA.** One CTA: watch the recording (on-demand link or `[LINK: on-demand recording]`), or the user's stated goal.

## Length and format

- Target 700 to 1,100 words; shorter is fine if there is little substance (see "Little substance to quote" in the edge cases).
- Pull quotes: up to 2, verbatim, under about 25 words each, attributed with name, title, and company from `list_speakers`.
- If the user gave a target keyword, use it in the title, the meta description, one H2, and naturally in the text. Don't force it.
- Internal or external links: only ones the user supplied. Otherwise `[LINK: ...]` placeholders.
- Format as headings and paragraphs the user can paste into a CMS.

## Multiple sessions

Write around the through-line across sessions and name the sessions where they support a point. A section-per-session roundup only if the user asks for one.

## Deliver

- Title options, meta description, then the post.
- Anything uncertain goes in `[VERIFY: ...]` and in Review notes.
